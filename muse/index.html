Overnight News — Intraday Paper Test

Nifty 500 universe · paper test only · first picks Wed 25 Nov 2026

Paper test only — no real money is at stake. This page records whether overnight news predicts a stock’s intraday direction. It is not investment advice.

How it works

  • Universe: Nifty 500 stocks.
  • News window: 4:00 pm on the last trading day to 8:30 am on the next trading day. Weekends and market holidays sit inside the window, so no news is missed.
  • Sources: Google News RSS (primary) and ET Markets RSS (supplement), plus exchange filings — the highest-signal source. Filings come from NSE's own announcements feed, read fresh each morning (latest filings, with NSE's summary). A mirror (Screener.in) was tried and dropped — it republishes filings about five days late. Moneycontrol is unusable as a primary source — it carries no publication timestamps.
  • Filtering: hard rules keep only real company news — results, orders, upgrades and downgrades, deals, approvals, incidents — and drop routine price pages, macro news and false matches. Namesake false positives (a cricket team, a foreign parent, a show sharing a name) are caught by an exclusion list that grows as new ones appear.
  • Scoring: hybrid, sentiment-based, and subjective. The shortlist is read and scored by judgement, −2 to +2, with a one-line reason. Size and context count: a large order beats a small one, a big-drug approval beats a small-drug one. No formula.
  • Picks: the 2–3 stories with the highest absolute score each morning, up or down. If the best story scores 0, there is no pick that day. Picks go out before 9:00 am.
  • Judging: each pick is logged with the 9:15 open price, the ~9:30 price and the close. A pick is right if the close moved in the predicted direction from the open. After 2–3 weeks we judge the hit rate — and whether 9:15 or 9:30 was the better entry.

Honest limits

  • Most of the 500 get no news on a given night — on the dry-run night, only 13 of the 500 had real overnight news. Picks skew to large caps and news-heavy days. Thin coverage is expected, not a failure.
  • Gap-ups usually fade from the open: earlier backtesting found stocks gapping up 2%+ end green 72% of the time but fall from the open on average. That is why this test is judged open-to-close, not on the gap.
  • Free news sources keep no months-long per-stock history, so the proof is this forward paper test, not a long backtest.
